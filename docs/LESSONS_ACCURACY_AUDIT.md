The catalogue is structurally complete, but “100% accurate” must not be treated
as a one-time claim. Biology changes, especially medicine, public health,
biotechnology, genomics, and research methods. Every lesson should retain a
source date and be re-reviewed when its content is expanded.

## Reference hierarchy

1. **Foundational biology:** OpenStax Biology 2e and NCBI Bookshelf university
   texts for cell biology, genetics, molecular biology, evolution, ecology,
   physiology, immunology, neuroscience, and developmental biology.
2. **Health and public health:** current WHO and CDC guidance.
3. **Research and laboratory methods:** NCBI/NIH references and method-specific
   primary literature.
4. **Taxonomy and nomenclature:** current specialist databases should be checked
   when a lesson gives a formal taxonomic placement.

## Editorial rules

- Do not call a hypothesis a fact.
- Do not say that evolution or adaptation has a goal.
- Do not say that individuals evolve during their lifetime.
- Do not imply that one gene always determines one trait.
- Separate correlation from causation.
- State the limits of models, tests, and diagnostic results.
- Do not give patient-specific diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Explain scientific disagreement and uncertainty when they are material.
- Date health guidance and re-check it against current public-health sources.
- Review every supplied diagram, inline image, label, scale, and colour convention
  against the final lesson text before release.
